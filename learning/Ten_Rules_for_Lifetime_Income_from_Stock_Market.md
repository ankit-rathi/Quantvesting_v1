The session outlines ten foundational rules for profitable stock trading, emphasizing that long-term success requires strict adherence to a disciplined, business-like approach rather than treating the market like a source of fixed income. Following these rules can help an investor achieve a **26% CAGR**, which doubles wealth every three years.

### **1. Adopt a Business Mindset**
Stock trading must be treated as a **business, not a fixed-salary profession**. In business, profits are irregular; there may be months with no deals and others with several. You should calculate your success based on annual averages rather than expecting a specific monthly payout.

### **2. Financial Discipline and Patient Capital**
*   **Invest Only "Patient" Money:** Only use capital that you do not need for at least **three years**. Short-term market behavior is unpredictable due to global scenarios, political shifts, and technological innovations.
*   **Avoid Debt:** Never use borrowed money for trading. High debt is the primary reason many large companies and individuals face financial ruin. You should only trade with your own capital that you can afford to hold for the long term.

### **3. Rigorous Analysis and Diversification**
*   **Buy Based on Analysis, Not Tips:** Every purchase should follow a **proper analysis** (business, financial, technical, and economic) or be based on consultation with a trusted expert. Avoid "colleague tips" or hearsay.
*   **The 5% Rule:** Never invest more than **5% of your total capital in a single stock**. Violating this rule—by concentrating 30-100% of capital in one stock—is the most common reason traders face total ruin.

### **4. Strategic Execution and Emotional Control**
*   **Pre-defined Exit Points:** You must decide your **selling price before you buy**. Whether you aim for a 10% or 20% gain, stick to that target rather than waiting indefinitely.
*   **Hold During Market Downturns:** If the market dips due to temporary external factors, do not panic-sell at a loss. Just as a shopkeeper holds inventory during a temporary disruption, you must hold your quality stocks until the market recovers.
*   **Zero Regret Policy:** If you sell a stock at your target profit and the price continues to rise, **do not feel regret**. Be happy that your analysis was correct and that you followed your discipline; once a stock is sold, stop tracking its performance to avoid psychological guilt.

### **5. Focus and Reinvestment**
*   **Avoid Sector Envy:** Every sector has its "season". Do not close your "shop" (sell your strategy) just because another sector is currently performing better; trust your own items and wait for your cycle.
*   **Compound Your Profits:** Instead of spending trading profits on luxuries like cars, **reinvest them back into your trading capital**. Wealth is built by increasing your capital base so that you can buy larger quantities of stocks over time. 
*   **Consistency over Novelty:** Once you find a strategy that works (e.g., specific moving averages), stick with it for years. There is no need to change a winning method unless the market structure fundamentally shifts.

### **The "All-or-Nothing" Principle**
The sources emphasize that these ten rules form a complete system. Following nine out of ten is not enough; **missing even one rule can cause the entire system to fail**. Success is a combination of this professional discipline and a daily practice of gratitude and seeking protection for one's "body, mind, and wealth".

***

**Analogy:**
Think of stock trading like **running a traditional clothing shop**. You don't sell your entire inventory at a loss just because a temporary flood closed the street for a week. You buy quality items you understand, you set a fair markup price before putting them on the shelf, and you don't shut down your clothing business to open a pharmacy just because you see a pharmacy is busy during a flu outbreak. Success comes from keeping your shop open, sticking to your specialty, and using your earnings to buy more inventory for next season.
